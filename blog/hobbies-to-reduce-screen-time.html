Hands-on hobbies to reduce screen time

If your days happen on a screen, a hands-on hobby gives your eyes and mind a break. Why making things works and easy ideas to start this weekend.

By Yana in Phuket · November 6, 2026

The short answer

The hobbies that pull you away from screens best are hands-on ones with a finished result, like cooking, pottery, gardening, knitting or making your own body care. They keep your hands busy, give you something to show for your time and are hard to do while scrolling.

Why is scrolling so hard to stop?

Phones are designed to be easy to pick up and hard to put down. When you're tired after work, scrolling asks nothing of you. That's why telling yourself to use your phone less rarely works. You need something else to reach for, something at least as satisfying.

Why hands-on hobbies work

  • Your hands are busy. It's hard to hold a phone while you're whisking, kneading or pouring.
  • There's a finished result. At the end you have something you made. Scrolling doesn't leave anything behind.
  • You focus on one thing. Weighing, stirring and waiting for the right temperature pull your attention back into the room.
  • It's sensory. Textures, scents and colors are a welcome change after hours of flat glass.

Many people describe a calm, absorbed feeling while they make things, when an hour passes without a glance at the clock. You don't need to be good at anything for it to happen.

Hands-on hobbies to try

  • Cooking and baking. Practical, and you get to eat the results.
  • Growing herbs. Even a windowsill pot of rosemary counts, and you can dry it later for an infused oil.
  • Pottery or polymer clay. Great for your hands and very forgiving for beginners.
  • Knitting, crochet or embroidery. Portable, repetitive and easy to pick up in short sessions.
  • Candle making. A good first project with few tools.
  • Making natural body care. Lip balm, body butter, deodorant or shampoo bars. You learn some simple science and end up with things you use every day.

How making replaced scrolling for me

I work in marketing, so most of my day happens on a screen. In 2022 I started looking for something to do with my hands. I tried polymer clay, then candles, then I took my first soap making workshop in Bangkok, and that's where natural body care began for me.

Making became my way to switch off after a day of screens. It's also how ARK Experience started.

Why natural body care makes a good screen-free hobby

  • It's useful. You end up with lip balm, body butter or deodorant you'll actually use, for yourself, your family and your friends.
  • It's quick. A batch of lip balm takes about 20 minutes and fills 20 tubes.
  • You learn as you make. Why a butter feels the way it does, what a wax adds and how temperature changes texture.
  • It makes great gifts. More on that in handmade body care gifts for friends.

How to make a new hobby stick

  • Pick a regular time, like Sunday morning or one evening a week.
  • Leave your tools where you'll see them.
  • Put your phone in another room while you make.
  • Start with a small project you can finish in one session.
  • Make something you'll use or give away, so it feels worth it.
