Handmade body care gifts for friends

Easy handmade gifts your friends will use, from lip balm to whipped body butter. Ideas, how far ahead to make them and how to wrap and label them.

By Yana in Phuket · November 6, 2026

The short answer

The easiest handmade body care gifts are lip balm, whipped body butter, infused body oil, natural deodorant and shampoo bars. They're quick to make in batches, keep well and feel personal. Add a label with the ingredients and the date, and a short note on how to use it.

Why body care makes a good handmade gift

It's useful, so it won't end up in a drawer. It's personal, because you chose the scent and made it with your own hands. And one batch gives you several gifts at once. Twenty tubes of lip balm covers a lot of birthdays.

Five gift ideas, from easiest to more involved

Lip balm

The easiest place to start. One batch takes about 20 minutes and fills twenty tubes. Tie three together with ribbon or tuck one into a card. My free lip balm guide has a tested formula, including a vegan option.

Infused body oil

Dried lavender, calendula or rosemary infused in a carrier oil and poured into a small amber bottle looks lovely and smells even better. Start a slow infusion about a month ahead, or make it the same day with gentle heat.

Whipped body butter

A jar of fluffy whipped body butter feels special. Make it close to the day and keep it somewhere cool, especially in a warm climate.

Natural deodorant

A more personal gift for a close friend who's curious about switching. Add a note that it's a deodorant, not an antiperspirant.

Shampoo bars

Plastic-free, travel-friendly and easy to wrap in paper. Pair a bar with a small wooden soap dish so it lasts longer.

How far ahead to make them

  • Lip balm, body butter and deodorant are best made a week or two before you give them, so they're fresh.
  • Shampoo bars need a day or two to harden after pressing.
  • Slow infused oils take about a month, so start early, or use the heat method if you're short on time.

Wrapping ideas

  • Kraft paper, twine and a sprig of dried rosemary
  • A small linen or cotton pouch
  • A clean glass jar you've reused, washed and dried completely
  • A small box with crinkle paper, holding two or three makes together

Always label your gifts

Write the product name, the ingredients and the date on each one. Your friend might have an allergy you don't know about, to nut oils and butters for example, or to essential oils. Add a line on how to use it and suggest a patch test. It's a small step that shows you care.

Gifts, not products

One honest note. Gifts are a lovely way to share what you make. Selling homemade cosmetics is different, and in many countries it needs registration, safety checks and specific labels. If you're thinking about selling, look up the rules where you live first.

Start making
