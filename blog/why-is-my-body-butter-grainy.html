Why is my body butter grainy?

Grainy body butter comes from how the butters cool, not from bad ingredients. What causes the bumps, how to fix a batch and how to prevent it.

By Yana in Phuket · November 6, 2026

The short answer

Grainy body butter happens when butters like shea cool slowly or unevenly. Their fats set at different temperatures and form small crystals that feel like sand. You can usually fix a batch by melting it fully, keeping it warm for a while and then cooling it quickly.

What causes the grains?

Plant butters such as shea, mango and cocoa butter aren't one single fat. Each is a mix of fatty acids that melt and set at different temperatures. When melted butter cools slowly, the fats with the highest melting point set first, on their own, and form little crystals. Those are the grains you feel.

They're not a sign that the butter has gone bad. A grainy batch is fine to use, and the grains melt as soon as they touch warm skin. They just don't feel nice, and they stop a whipped butter from looking smooth.

Shea butter is the most common cause, but any butter can do it.

The usual triggers

  • Cooling too slowly. Leaving melted butter on the counter for hours gives crystals time to grow.
  • Not melting it fully. If a few crystals survive the melt, they act as seeds for new ones.
  • Temperature swings after making. A jar that warms up in the sun and cools down at night can turn grainy weeks later. This happens a lot in hot climates and during shipping.
  • Butter that arrived grainy. Some unrefined shea is already grainy in the bag. Melting it properly solves this.

How to fix a grainy batch

  1. Scoop the butter back into a heatproof bowl and melt it gently in a double boiler.
  2. Once everything is liquid, keep it warm for a little while, so every crystal melts, not only the ones you can see.
  3. Cool it quickly. A bowl set in ice water, or a short rest in the fridge, helps the fats set together in small, even crystals.
  4. Whip it once it's firm at the edges and still soft in the middle, then spoon it into clean, dry jars.

If your butter had essential oils in it, some of the scent may fade when you melt it again. That's normal.

How to prevent it next time

  • Melt everything fully, and give it a little time warm before you cool it.
  • Cool it fast and on purpose, never slowly on the counter.
  • Store your jars somewhere cool and steady, away from sun and steamy bathrooms.
  • In a hot climate, keep one jar in use and the rest in a cooler spot.

Is grainy body butter still OK to use?

Yes. Graininess changes texture, not safety. If your butter smells sharp or like crayons, or looks and smells different from when you made it, that's oxidation, which is a different story. Then it's time for a fresh batch.
